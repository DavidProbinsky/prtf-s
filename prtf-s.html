PRTF-S GUIDE

How the score works

The six metrics split into two groups. Four describe how easy the finding is to exploit. Two describe how bad the outcome is. Impact sets the ceiling: an easy finding with a minor outcome stays low, and how easy it is only decides how close the score gets to that ceiling.

E = (AV + AC + DL + SE) ÷ 4
I = (2 × the higher of SC and CI + the lower) ÷ 3
Score = 1 + (I − 1) × (E + 2) ÷ 6, rounded to one decimal
If CI is X (physical harm possible), the score is at least 2.5

E and I both run from 1 to 4. Because of the formula, the score can never be higher than I. Every metric value is a whole number from 1 to 4, so two assessors who agree on the metrics always get the same score.

Severity levels

ScoreSeverityWhat it usually means
1.0 – 1.6LOWLimited outcome. Fix during normal maintenance.
1.7 – 2.4MEDIUMReal weakness with a contained outcome. Plan a fix.
2.5 – 3.2HIGHSerious outcome that is realistic to reach. Prioritize.
3.3 – 4.0CRITICALSevere outcome that is easy to reach. Act now.

Vector string

Write a score as a vector so anyone can check it:

PRTF-S:2.0/AV:E/AC:N/DL:L/SC:F/CI:C/SE:N = 2.8 (HIGH)

In a report, put it next to the PRTF technique: PRTF-6.06 Tailgating & Piggybacking · PRTF-S 2.8 HIGH.

Metric reference

AV · Access Vector (exploitability)

Where does the attacker start?

  • PPerimeter (1). Must breach fencing, gates, or outer barriers before reaching the building.
  • EExterior (2). Attack possible from the building exterior or publicly accessible grounds.
  • IInterior (3). Attacker is already inside the facility. No perimeter or exterior breach needed.
  • UUnrestricted (4). No barrier exists. The target is openly reachable by anyone.
AC · Access Complexity (exploitability)

How much effort does exploitation take?

  • HHigh (1). Specialized tools or advanced skill required. Not an opportunistic attack.
  • MMedium (2). Basic preparation or common tools needed. Achievable with modest effort.
  • LLow (3). Minimal skill, no special tools. Most attackers can do this reliably.
  • NNone (4). Zero effort. Anyone can exploit this with no preparation or tools.
DL · Detection Likelihood (exploitability)

How likely is the attacker to be detected?

  • HHigh (1). Active monitoring. Cameras, guards, or staff would likely detect and respond.
  • MMedium (2). Some monitoring exists, but gaps allow execution without guaranteed detection.
  • LLow (3). Monitoring is sparse or inactive. Low risk of being seen.
  • NNone (4). No effective detection. The attacker can operate freely.
SE · Social Engineering Required (exploitability)

Was social engineering needed?

  • CComplex (1). Elaborate pretext, props, or a multi-step deception campaign required.
  • AActive (2). Must engage, build rapport, and actively deceive one or more employees.
  • PPassive (3). Attacker only needs to blend in. No conversation or active deception.
  • NNone (4). No social engineering required. Purely physical or technical weakness.
SC · Scope of Compromise (impact)

What access is gained?

  • LLimited (1). One room or small contained area. Impact is local and isolated.
  • ZZone (2). Extends to a floor, wing, or defined security zone within the facility.
  • FFacility (3). Full facility access achievable. All areas reachable from this entry point.
  • MMulti-site (4). Access across multiple sites or systemic shared infrastructure.
CI · Consequence Impact (impact)

What is the worst realistic outcome?

  • IInfo (1). Low-sensitivity information or a non-critical area. Easily contained.
  • DDisruption (2). Operations disrupted, equipment tampered with, or staff safety put at risk.
  • CCompromise (3). Sensitive data, credentials, or critical IT systems accessed or taken.
  • XCatastrophic (4). Physical harm to people or irreversible facility damage is possible.

Scoring guidance

  • Score what you observed during the assessment, not the worst thing you can imagine. If you did not confirm an outcome, pick the impact you can support with evidence.
  • Score each finding on its own. If two findings chain together, score the chain as its own finding and say so.
  • When more than one assessor scores the same finding, review any metric where the values differ by more than one point before finalizing. The goal is scoring you can defend and repeat.
  • PRTF-S measures severity, not total business risk. A client may weigh the same score differently depending on what the site protects.
  • Scores are advisory. They support a professional judgment, they do not replace it.

Changelog

v2.0 · Nov 24, 2026

  • Renamed from PS-CVSS to PRTF Severity Score (PRTF-S) to pair with the Physical Red Team Framework and to avoid confusion with CVSS.
  • New formula. v1.1 averaged all six metrics, so effort counted for two thirds of the score and impact for one third. An unlocked supply closet could score HIGH, and a finding that could physically hurt someone could score LOW. In v2.0 impact sets the ceiling, and CI:X is always at least HIGH.
  • Same six metrics, same letters, same 1 to 4 values. Vectors written for v1.1 still load; the score is recalculated with v2.0.
  • Released as an open resource under CC BY 4.0 with an interactive calculator and vector strings.

v1.1 · Mar 24, 2026 (as PS-CVSS)

  • Two-page PDF reference. Score = sum of the six metrics ÷ 6.
